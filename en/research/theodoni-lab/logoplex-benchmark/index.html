Linguistic Computation & LLM Limitations

The Logoplex Benchmark

The question

Large language models (LLMs) use language with impressive fluency. But can they extract its rules?

To what extent do they acquire productive knowledge of rules, and where do they rely on memorising frequent patterns?

Building Logoplex across typologically diverse languages (Ancient and Modern Greek, Chinese, Arabic and more), the limits show most where everyday speech goes beyond strict grammar, as well as in rare forms, stress and dialects. Is this a matter of data, of how models learn, or is part of living language simply beyond rules?

The approach

Logoplex is not only a language-learning game; it is a living research testbed. To work at all, each language is encoded in explicit rules, which become a yardstick for both models and native speakers.

  • Everyday speech & constructions: where living language escapes the rules, and how models handle it compared with people.
  • Rule or memory: tests with pseudo-words, to see whether a rule is applied to words the model has never seen.
  • Comparing languages and models: matched items in Greek, English and other languages, and models with the same architecture but different training data, to separate what is due to data from what is due to language.
  • Open science: test sets and results will be published openly.

Example thesis topics: rule or memory with pseudo-words (Greek and English) · data, tokenizer or model size? · everyday speech, dialects and Katharevousa · rule induction from an invented language.
