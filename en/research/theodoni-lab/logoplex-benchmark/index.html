Linguistic Computation & LLM Limitations

The Logoplex Benchmark

The question

By developing Logoplex across typologically diverse languages (Ancient and Modern Greek, Chinese, Arabic and more), we study a fundamental scientific question:

Large language models excel at statistical imitation of language. But to what extent do they extract strict, compositional rules of syntax and grammar, and where do they fail?

And where they fail: is it a matter of data and training, of neural network architecture, or of the intrinsic complexity of human language itself?

The approach

Logoplex is not only a language-learning game; it is a living research testbed. To work at all, each language has to be encoded in explicit rules: which words combine, how they inflect, when a sentence is grammatical. Those rules become the yardstick against which language-model outputs are compared.

  • Comparison: LLM outputs against explicit grammars, in languages with very different structures.
  • Benchmark: building syntax test sets, starting with Greek.
  • Theory: what model errors tell us about the nature of language and cognition.
